# 3.1.2 Data Gathering Technique

Consistent with the descriptive-developmental research design and Agile (Scrum-based) iterative development model in Section 3.1.1, data gathering supports the specification, refinement and evaluation of L.A.R.A. Documentary analysis establishes the classroom requirements, while questionnaires, Learner task records and technical measurements describe the system's usability, software quality and operation. The procedures below concern the proposed pilot public school and do not represent completed field findings.

| Method or instrument | Information gathered | Respondents or evidence source |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements and document review | Classroom activities, role responsibilities, assessment procedures and offline requirements | Classroom workflow descriptions, system requirements and design documents |
| System Usability Scale (SUS) | Perceived usability following practical use | 15 Teachers |
| ISO/IEC 25010-based questionnaire | Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability and Reliability | 3 to 5 ICT experts |
| Learner task-performance record | Successful task completion, elapsed task time and assistance required | The approximately 40 Learners in the pilot class |
| Technical measurement record | Offline operation, resource use, response time, submission accuracy and recovery | Test devices, Hub logs and stored records |

*Table 3.x. Data-gathering methods, instruments and evidence sources.*

**Requirements analysis.** Documentary analysis examines the classroom responsibilities of the Learner, Teacher and Admin, including enrollment, learning-resource distribution, homework, assessment and Hub administration. Requirements and design documents identify the functions and operating constraints examined during development. The analysis does not assert that interviews or classroom observations were conducted before development.

**Questionnaire administration.** The researchers administer the questionnaires after participants have used the relevant system functions. Teachers complete the ten-item SUS after practical classroom use. ICT experts evaluate the four selected software-quality dimensions after examining the system and its demonstrated workflows. Responses are recorded on questionnaire forms and checked for completeness before scoring; incomplete response sets are identified separately rather than assigned invented ratings. Participation and the handling of evaluation records follow school authorization and the applicable consent arrangements.

The SUS uses five response positions from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Following Brooke's scoring procedure, one is subtracted from each odd-numbered item rating, and each even-numbered item rating is subtracted from five. The ten adjusted values are summed and multiplied by 2.5, producing an individual score from 0 to 100. The mean is calculated across complete Teacher response sets and compared with the usability target of 80.0. The score is a composite usability measure, not a percentage of correct responses.

The software-quality questionnaire uses five-point agreement ratings. For each item, the weighted mean is calculated as WM = sum of the products of each rating and its frequency, divided by the total number of valid responses. Item means are summarized within each dimension, and mean scores and standard deviations describe the expert ratings. Table 3.x defines equal-width interpretation bands; these are questionnaire interpretation rules rather than measured outcomes or a claim of ISO certification.

| Rating | Response | Weighted-mean interval | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Strongly agree | 4.21 to 5.00 | Strong agreement |
| 4 | Agree | 3.41 to 4.20 | Agreement |
| 3 | Neither agree nor disagree | 2.61 to 3.40 | Neutral response |
| 2 | Disagree | 1.81 to 2.60 | Disagreement |
| 1 | Strongly disagree | 1.00 to 1.80 | Strong disagreement |

*Table 3.x. Five-point questionnaire response coding and interpretation of means rounded to two decimal places.*

The quality evaluation includes Performance Efficiency alongside Functional Suitability, Usability and Reliability. The quality target of 4.50 out of 5.00 provides a comparison point for the evaluation; actual dimension scores are reported independently and are not adjusted to meet a target.

**Learner task performance.** The researchers observe the pilot class while Learners perform representative activities such as opening a class, reading saved materials, submitting homework and completing an available quiz. The task record identifies whether the assigned activity is completed, elapsed time from the start instruction to completion, and any assistance or interruption. Completion rate expresses successful attempts as a proportion of recorded attempts. Incomplete tasks and assisted attempts remain identifiable in the records. Evaluation concerns the single pilot class and does not divide Learners into separate grade-group comparison samples.

**Technical measurements.** Technical testing examines the classroom cycle on an isolated classroom network and the preservation of work when the Hub becomes unreachable. The researchers record the test conditions, expected outcomes, observed behavior and failures. Simulated clients provide technical load and are distinct from the human respondent sample.

| Planned measurement | Procedure or recorded evidence | Evaluation target |
|---|---|---|
| Offline classroom cycle | Exercise classroom functions with the router's external-network connection unplugged; reopen downloaded resources with Wi-Fi off | No failures caused by the absence of an external connection |
| Mobile resource use | Measure application heap and out-of-memory events on a phone with 3 to 4 GB RAM during document, video, camera, upload and Hub-assisted tutor activities | Heap below 250 MB and no out-of-memory crashes |
| AI response performance | Record the serving request's time to first token and generation rate, distinguishing queue waiting time | First token below 2.5 seconds and generation at 8 to 15 tokens per second |
| Simultaneous quiz submissions | Compare expected scores and receipts with records from 40 simultaneous submissions | Complete and accurate score recording |
| Homework upload and reconnection | Upload 40 homework photos and repeat with disconnection and queued delivery | Successful uploads and no lost work |
| Backup and restoration | Compare expected records with restored records after interruption | Recovery without corrupted records |
| Initial Hub installation | Record elapsed setup time on a fresh Hub computer | Installation in under three minutes without command-line steps |
| Paperless activity comparison | Compare paper quantity and reproduction cost for equivalent printed and digital activities | Elimination of printing costs for the replaced materials |

*Table 3.x. Planned technical measurements and comparison targets.*

Stress-test scenarios are repeated five times, with response times and success counts obtained from logs and stored records. Device details and operating conditions accompany Mobile resource measurements. Questionnaire responses, task records and technical measurements support descriptive evaluation of the system.
