# 3.1.2 Data Gathering Technique

Consistent with the descriptive-developmental research design, using an Agile (Scrum-based) iterative development model presented in Section 3.1.1, data gathering will support the specification, refinement and evaluation of L.A.R.A. Sprint 0 through Sprint 6 comprise parallel Hub, Mobile app and Desktop app workstreams, with shared contracts, review and integration at sprint boundaries. Documentary evidence will support iterative refinement, while the planned evaluation will combine Teacher-reported usability, software-quality ratings, Learner task performance and technical measurements. These procedures describe intended data collection; they do not establish that a school visit, questionnaire administration or pilot evaluation has occurred.

| Method or instrument | Information obtained or intended to be obtained | Participants or evidence source | Role in the study |
|---|---|---|---|
| Requirements analysis and document review | Classroom workflows, role responsibilities, offline requirements and evaluation criteria | Project requirements, shared interface definitions, design records and technical specifications | Establish the documented basis for development and evaluation; the original requirements-gathering history remains to be confirmed. |
| System Usability Scale (SUS) | Overall perceived usability after system use | At least 15 DepEd elementary Teachers, as planned | Evaluate Teacher experience against the project's usability target. |
| ISO/IEC 25010-based five-point questionnaire | Ratings of Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability and Reliability | Respondent group awaiting confirmation | Identify perceived software-quality strengths and areas requiring improvement. |
| Learner task-performance records | Task completion and time on task, grouped as Grades 1–3 and Grades 4–6 | Participating Learners within the approved evaluation sample | Examine effectiveness and efficiency during system use. |
| Technical measurements and test records | Offline operation, device resource use, response time, data accuracy and recovery | Test devices, the Hub, load-script logs and database checks | Compare observed operation with the project's technical targets. |

**Requirements analysis and documentary evidence.** The project requirements describe the classroom responsibilities of the Learner, Teacher and Admin, including enrollment, learning-resource distribution, assignments, quizzes and Hub administration. The shared interface definitions specify supported exchanges and records, while the Mobile app technical specification describes offline access and operation on resource-constrained phones. These documents provide the basis for identifying the functions and constraints to be examined during evaluation. They are design evidence rather than records of participant responses. The design record also documents a completed review of Google Classroom's desktop interface structure and computed layout measurements; it explicitly states that phone layouts were not measured. This comparative interface analysis informed screen organization and does not constitute classroom observation or evidence of consultation with Teachers or Learners.

**Teacher usability questionnaire.** The field-evaluation plan specifies a ten-item SUS questionnaire for at least 15 DepEd elementary Teachers after live classroom testing. It will gather perceptions of overall usability following practical experience with L.A.R.A. This number is a recruitment target rather than an achieved sample. The proposed implementation sequence places evaluation after classroom use, in relative Week 5, with Developers and participating Teachers arranging the activities. It does not identify the person administering each instrument or establish calendar dates.

Responses to the standard SUS use five positions from strong disagreement to strong agreement. For each completed response set, one will be subtracted from each odd-numbered item rating, and each even-numbered item rating will be subtracted from five. The ten adjusted values will be summed and multiplied by 2.5, producing an individual score from 0 to 100. The mean will be calculated across scored Teacher response sets. This procedure follows Brooke's SUS scoring method.[^sus] The observed mean will be reported alongside the project target of at least 80.0; the target is not a finding. SUS will provide a composite usability measure rather than a separate quality rating for each item.

**Software-quality questionnaire.** The evaluation plan specifies a five-point Likert questionnaire based on ISO/IEC 25010, covering Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability and Reliability. The instrument will gather ratings of the corresponding aspects of L.A.R.A. following evaluation use. These ratings will complement measured task performance and technical tests. The questionnaire is a planned evaluation instrument; its inclusion does not establish ISO certification, instrument validation or coverage of the entire standard.

For each item, the frequency-weighted mean will be calculated as `WM = Σ(f_r × r) / Σf_r`, where `r` is an approved numeric rating from 1 to 5 and `f_r` is the frequency of that rating. The evaluation will report the observed means and standard deviations, as specified in the survey plan. The PRD sets a mean target of at least 4.50 out of 5.00 across its listed quality dimensions. The approved method for combining item scores into dimension and overall scores remains to be established.

**Learner task-performance evaluation.** The planned evaluation will record task completion rates and time on task for Learners in Grades 1–3 and Grades 4–6. Completion rate will express successfully completed task attempts as a proportion of the task attempts included under the approved scoring rules. Elapsed task time will indicate the time required to perform the assigned activity. Grouped records will inform the assessment of interaction difficulties and possible refinements to navigation and classroom workflows. The elementary grade groupings belong to the planned evaluation and do not limit the system's wider Grades 1–12 product scope.

**Technical data collection.** Technical measurements will examine the classroom cycle on an isolated classroom network and the preservation of work when the Hub becomes unreachable. Measurements will be compared with the targets below; no target is treated as an achieved outcome.

| Planned measurement | Information to be collected | Project target |
|---|---|---|
| Offline classroom cycle | Completion of the cycle with the router's WAN connection unplugged | Full operation without failures caused by absence of an external connection |
| Mobile stability | Heap use and out-of-memory events on phones with 3–4 GB RAM during Hub-assisted AI use | Heap below 250 MB and no out-of-memory crashes |
| AI response performance | Time to first token and generation rate | Time to first token below 2.5 seconds on the Hub queue and generation at 8–15 tokens per second |
| Concurrent quiz submissions | Expected and recorded scores and submission receipts for 40 simultaneous submissions | Complete and accurate score recording |
| Homework submission and reconnection | Successful uploads across 40 concurrent submissions, retained queued work and delivery after reconnection | All expected uploads succeed; no lost work after interruption |
| Backup and restoration | Expected records compared with restored records after the planned power-loss scenario | Full recovery without corrupted records |
| Initial Hub installation | Elapsed installation time and required setup actions on a fresh Hub PC | Installation in under three minutes without command-line steps |
| Paperless activity comparison | Paper quantity and reproduction cost for equivalent simulated printed and digital quiz and worksheet activities | Elimination of printing costs for the materials replaced by the digital workflow |

The Hub testing method specifies five repetitions per stress-test scenario, with response times and success counts obtained from load-script logs and expected records checked against the database. Simulated clients in those tests will provide technical load rather than human questionnaire responses. Mobile resource measurements will concern the documented budget-phone environment. The paperless comparison will concern the equivalent simulated activities rather than assumed annual savings. These records will inform assessment of technical suitability and subsequent refinements.
