# 3.1.3 Population and Sample Size

The study involves the pilot public school and one pilot class of about 40 Learners. Consistent with the descriptive-developmental research design and Agile (Scrum-based) iterative development model in Section 3.1.1, the evaluation includes Teachers, Learners and ICT experts who examine the completed classroom workflows. The school is described without naming it, and the planned sample identifies intended participants rather than completed recruitment.

| Respondent group | Population | Planned sample | Sampling technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teachers | Teachers associated with the pilot public school who use the system for classroom activities | 15 Teachers for the System Usability Scale | Purposive sampling |
| Learners | All Learners enrolled in the selected pilot class | Approximately 40 Learners, covering the whole class | Total enumeration |
| ICT experts | Individuals with relevant expertise in software development, information systems or system evaluation | 3 to 5 ICT experts for the ISO/IEC 25010-based evaluation | Purposive sampling |

*Table 3.x. Respondent groups, intended samples and sampling techniques.*

**Teacher respondents.** Fifteen Teachers are selected purposively because usability ratings require experience with the system's classroom functions. Their participation provides the basis for the ten-item System Usability Scale after practical use. The selection concerns relevance to the evaluation and does not claim a random sample representative of every Teacher in Philippine public schools.

**Learner respondents.** Total enumeration includes the approximately 40 Learners of the pilot class. Including the entire class supports observation of the intended classroom cycle without selecting only Learners who find the application easy to use. Task-completion and time-on-task records describe this class. The study does not compare separate lower- and upper-elementary grade groups.

**ICT expert respondents.** Three to five ICT experts are selected purposively for their relevant technical knowledge and ability to evaluate Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability and Reliability. Their software-quality ratings complement Teacher usability responses and Learner task records; the three respondent groups have distinct evaluation roles.

The sample is determined by the pilot setting and respondent responsibilities rather than a statistical population-estimation formula. No Slovin calculation is applied to purposive selection or to enumeration of the whole pilot class. School authorization, voluntary participation and appropriate consent or assent arrangements precede data collection involving Teachers and minor Learners. Records used for analysis are handled without exposing personal classroom identifiers.

The planned sample is distinct from the 40 simulated clients used in technical load testing. Simulated submissions measure system behavior and are not human questionnaire responses. Actual participant counts, complete response sets and respondent distributions are reported in Chapter IV.
